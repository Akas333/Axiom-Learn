# Stage 1 — intuition

## The basic idea first, in plain words

Open the concept with its **basic idea**: one short paragraph, in plain, physical, picture-able language, that says what the law or idea *does*. No equations, no symbols, no jargon he doesn't hold yet. It should paint a picture he can hold in his head and reason with.

The model to match (Ampère's law):

> Imagine an imaginary closed loop around a current-carrying wire. Add up the "push" of the magnetic field all the way around that loop — its circulation. That total is directly proportional to the amount of electric current piercing through the middle of the loop.

That is the whole idea, and nothing else. The formula, the conventions, the conditions all come later, in the section.

## Then make him use it — at most 3 questions

Ask prediction questions with the quizzing tool (`type: "predict"`, `nodeId` = the map node the idea underlies) that make him **apply the picture to a new situation** he hasn't been told about:

- scale something ("double the current — what happens to the circulation?"),
- change the geometry ("make the loop twice as big, same wire through it — does the total change?"),
- find the case where it gives zero or flips sign ("move the wire outside the loop — now?").

Each question is a nudge toward something the section will make precise (Principle ii: how could he have discovered it?). Its options are the answers a specific wrong picture would give — the miss tells you which picture he holds.

- **He answers correctly → the stage is done.** Move straight on to the section.
- **He misses → restate the picture in one line**, aimed at the wrong model his answer revealed, and ask a *different* question (never the same one again).
- **Three questions at most.** After the third, move on regardless — the section resolves what is still open, starting from his attempts.

When you open the section, resolve his predictions in a sentence or two: what he predicted, what is true, why.
